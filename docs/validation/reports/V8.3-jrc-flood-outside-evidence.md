# Report V8.3: JRC flood maps beside outside evidence

Date: 2026-11-17 · crc-sdk 0.11.1, crc-framework 0.6.0 · awaiting review

Stage 3 of the plan of 2026-11-17. JRC's GloFAS flood maps are set beside FEMA flood zones, USGS
gauge records and the high-water marks of Hurricane Harvey, in the Houston, Austin and Fremont
boxes. These are comparisons, not checks: numbers with their limits, no outcome, nothing added to
the tallies; the validation status of `jrc-flood` stays "not assessed". They describe the
publisher's maps, not crc-sdk's fidelity (that is [V8.1](V8.1-jrc-flood-verification.md)).
Committed locally on `main`; nothing is pushed. The Rhine was left out, as planned.

## How to look

- Numbers, methods and limits: `just validation-draft-preview jrc-flood`, section "Comparisons with
  outside evidence (no verdict)" (six comparisons, each folded).
- Map: `/validation/explore/`, river flood source, layers named "Evidence: ..." in the Houston,
  Austin and Fremont groups. The source "Flood evidence: gauges and Harvey marks" holds the points;
  with the flood source open they can be switched on under "Also show".

- Two thirds of the marks are where GloFAS has no water even at 500 years. Harvey flooded bayous,

- Where GloFAS has water, its 500-year depth is above the mark at 170 of 215 marks, by a median of
  1.2 m. A quarter of the marks have a height of 0 m (a line at ground level).
- How rare Harvey was differs by place. The USGS report gives annual exceedance probabilities from
  below 0.2 to 14 percent at 74 gauges; in this box, 19 of the 41 gauges it lists had a peak rarer
  than 1 percent a year, and one rarer than 0.2 percent. The plan's wording "Harvey exceeded a
  100-year flood" does not hold as a general statement and is not used.

## Limits common to all three

- GloFAS is a global model of large rivers without defences on a 90 m terrain model. None of the
  three kinds of evidence measures the same thing.
- FEMA zones are regulatory maps with dates per panel; the zone polygons were generalised by the
  service and some are not valid polygons after that (431 of 21,289 in Houston, 606 of 7,071 in
  Austin, 1 of 718 in Fremont).
- A gauge stage is a height above the gauge's datum. "Above flood stage" stands in for depth over
  the banks. Whether the NWS and USGS stage scales share a datum at each gauge was not checked.
- Marks are where surveyors went. Both rivers are regulated.
- Each comparison's own limits are in the record and on the preview page.

## Things you should know

- **The Harvey marks service.** USGS's old address (`stn.wim.usgs.gov`) now redirects to a page that
  answers 403. The data came from `api.waterdata.usgs.gov/stnservices`, an address the subagent found
  in the code of USGS's public Flood Event Viewer, not in documentation. It answered without a key;
  the viewer sends one. It is public USGS data, but the route is undocumented and may change.
- **Personal names.** The marks service names the people who flagged and surveyed each mark. The
  parser drops those fields; the saved test replies have them replaced.
- **FEMA licence.** The service states none and FEMA's web page refused the request; "a U.S. federal
  government work" is general knowledge, not something read from FEMA.
- **Requests** carried a generic client name and nothing identifying you or the project.

## Departures from the plan

- The plan named a package `evidence/` and a `comparisons` list; both exist. Evidence was also
  added to the map as cell shares and points, as the plan's step 5 said.
- The statement about Harvey's return period was replaced by what the cited report says.
- USGS peaks were used with NWS flood stages; the plan did not mention NWS.
- The FEMA comparison leaves out JRC's permanent water, found in Stage 2.
